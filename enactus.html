Leading a team of 40+

Enactus is a global network of students who use entrepreneurship to tackle social and environmental problems. As project manager at Enactus ESB, I led a team of more than 40 members working on projects aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

Keeping that many people moving in the same direction was the biggest challenge, and the biggest lesson. I learned to set clear goals, divide the work so everyone knew what they owned, follow up without micromanaging, and keep the team motivated, especially since everyone was a volunteer balancing Enactus with their studies.

Design thinking in practice

Every project started with people, not ideas. We used design thinking tools such as empathy mapping and ideation workshops to understand the people we wanted to help and define the real problem before jumping to solutions.

I ran many of these workshops myself. Getting a large group to share ideas openly, then narrowing them down to the ones worth building, taught me how to facilitate, listen and guide a discussion toward a decision.

From a problem to a product

The most valuable part of the experience was seeing a project through its whole life: spotting a problem, researching it, generating ideas, building a solution and turning it into something real. Few experiences as a student let you follow that full journey.

To keep it on track, I used project management tools and methods to plan timelines, manage budgets, track progress and coordinate with partners and stakeholders. I learned how to break a big goal into steps, anticipate risks and adjust the plan when reality didn't match it.

What I learned

  • LeadershipHow to guide, motivate and support a large team of volunteers.
  • CoordinationKeeping many people, tasks and partners aligned toward one goal.
  • Building productsTaking an idea from a real problem all the way to a working solution.
