BlightX

A natural, locally made spray that protects Tunisian pear orchards from fire blight, the disease that destroyed most of the country's pear production.

The problem

Fire blight is a bacterial disease that attacks pear and apple trees. Since it was first found in Tunisia in 2012, it has spread across the main growing regions and cost the country around 80% of its pear sector, with production falling from about 60,000 to 20,000 tons.

For farmers, an infection can mean cutting down and burning years of work. One farmer we met lost more than a third of his orchard within days, and the land produced nothing for two years.

What if the answer was already beneath our feet?

Our solution

BlightX is a 100% biological, water-soluble powder made from Tunisian materials. Farmers mix it with water and spray it on their trees. It dries into a thin, breathable film that seals the blossoms, branches and wounds where the bacteria get in.

The formula combines natural clay as a physical barrier, potassium silicate to strengthen the tree's own defenses, and pine and tea tree oils as natural antibacterials. A 1 kg pack treats 60 trees for a full season and costs less than chemical alternatives.

From problem to product

  1. Field researchInterviewed 30 farmers across Béja, Bizerte and Zaghouan, travelling more than 1,000 km.
  2. Research and developmentTwo months of lab work with experts from INRAT, Tunisia's national agricultural research institute.
  3. TestingFive lab and field tests under Tunisian conditions, bringing disease severity down from 85% to 2%.
  4. Going to marketSeven sales so far, five active clients and 25 hectares protected.

Impact

Clients report a 70% gain in productivity and more than 35,000 DT in combined savings. Because the formula is fully biological and locally sourced, it leaves no chemical residue and uses about half the water of conventional sprays. The project supports UN Sustainable Development Goals 2, 8, 9, 12 and 13.

What's next

BlightX is competing at the Enactus Tunisia National Expos 2026. By the end of 2027, the goal is to reach 60 farmers and protect 300 hectares across Tunisia, then expand to Algeria and the rest of North Africa.
