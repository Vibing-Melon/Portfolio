Jan – Jun 2026 · Université Côte d'Azur, Nice, France

Erasmus+ exchange semester

Six months in Nice on a full Erasmus+ scholarship: new people, new ways of thinking, and my first time building a life on my own in another country.

Meeting people from everywhere

From January to June 2026, I left Tunisia to spend a semester at Université Côte d'Azur through the Erasmus+ programme. I was one of the few students selected for the exchange, with a full scholarship, and I'm grateful for the trust that came with it.

The richest part of the experience was the people. My classmates and friends came from different countries, cultures and academic backgrounds, and each of them brought their own way of seeing the world.

Working and living alongside them taught me that there is rarely one "normal" way to do things. The way people plan, debate, make decisions or even split a bill depends on where they come from. Learning to notice those differences, instead of assuming everyone thinks like me, made me a better listener and a better teammate.

Learning from different perspectives

In class, group projects with international students meant every problem was looked at from several angles at once. Someone would raise a point I had never considered; I would bring an angle from my own experience in Tunisia that others hadn't thought of. The result was almost always stronger than what any of us would have produced alone.

Studying in a French university also meant adjusting to a different way of teaching and of being evaluated. It pushed me out of habits I didn't know I had, and showed me how much the context you learn in shapes the way you think.

Adaptability and independence

Living on my own in a student residence in Nice, I was responsible for everything: paperwork, budgeting, getting around a new city, and organising my days between classes, studying and the rest of life. Nobody was there to fix problems for me, so I learned to solve them myself, quickly and calmly.

Things didn't always go to plan, and that was part of the lesson. Each unexpected situation made me more flexible and more confident in my ability to land somewhere new and find my footing.

What I brought back

  • Open-mindednessCuriosity about how other people think, and the habit of asking before assuming.
  • AdaptabilityComfort with new environments, new rules and plans that change.
  • IndependenceConfidence to manage my life, my time and my problems on my own.
