SMU Career Fair, reimagined

I restructured the whole career fair experience, and built a web app to end the long, unpredictable queues.

Why it needed rethinking

At a typical career fair, students wander between stands, wait in long lines without knowing how long they'll take, and often leave before meeting the companies they came for. Recruiters see crowds at some stands and empty time at others. Everyone loses time, and the fair stops doing its one job: connecting students with employers.

The new career fair pipeline

Nine steps take each student from the front door to an interview.

  1. Welcome deskGet the fair booklet: companies, their opportunities (internships, PFE projects, graduate jobs) and how to apply.
  2. CV reviewHR professionals and career advisors show you what to fix and what already works.
  3. Improve and printUpdate your CV on a computer and print copies at the on-campus printing booth.
  4. Explore the boothsMeet company representatives and find the ones that match you. Interviews happen on campus.
  5. Join the queueScan the company's QR code, identify yourself in the web app and get a digital ticket.
  6. Track your positionYour phone shows your ticket, position, people ahead and estimated wait, so you can keep exploring.
  7. Staff manage interviewsBadged staff on both floors mark interviews as done, and the queue moves forward automatically.
  8. Your turnYou get an “It’s your turn!” notification with the floor and room.
  9. InterviewPresent your printed CV, have a short interview with the recruiter and finish with a handshake.

The web app: manage your queue

The heart of the new fair is a web app I vibe coded to manage the queues. Instead of standing in line, a student joins a company's queue from their phone and gets a ticket number.

The app shows their place in line, how many people are ahead and an estimate of the wait. As the line moves, it updates live, so students can visit other stands, prepare or take a break, and come back right when it's their turn.

What changes

Students spend their time talking to recruiters instead of standing in lines. Recruiters get a steady flow of candidates instead of crowds followed by empty stands. And the organisers can see, in real time, where people are waiting and which companies draw the most interest.
