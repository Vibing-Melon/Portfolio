TimeTrace

A quiet way to stay aware of your time, without a heavy productivity system.

Download TimeTrace Try it in your browser

Who it's for

TimeTrace is for anyone who loses track of time and wants to stay aware of it without a heavy productivity system. It suits people who work or study alone, like freelancers, students and remote workers, and anyone who wants to notice how they spend their hours without being nagged.

I built it for myself, to keep an honest eye on my own days.

How it works

Keep the page open while you work. Every so often, ideally within the hour, glance at the timer to see how long it has been since you last stopped to think. When you check in, write a line about what you did and say whether it was productive.

The background color shows how long you've gone without checking in, so drifting for two hours without noticing gets harder.

What it helps with

  • Spotting driftThe color shift and the one-hour and two-hour chimes show when hours have slipped by unnoticed.
  • Building a habit of reflectionWriting a line about what you did makes you look back on your time instead of letting it blur together.
  • Seeing patternsThe calendar scores each day from your yes and no answers. After a few weeks, you can see which days and stretches of time went well.
  • Reviewing laterThe history keeps every entry, marked productive or not, so you can look back at a week or a day.

Get TimeTrace

TimeTrace is a single file. Download it, double-click to open it in your browser and keep the tab open while you work. It works offline, and there's nothing to install or sign up for.
